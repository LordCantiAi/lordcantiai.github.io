1. Asset Overview

XRP is the native token of the XRP Ledger (XRPL), a decentralized blockchain designed for fast, low-cost cross-border payments and settlement. Created in 2012, XRP is closely associated with Ripple Labs, which builds enterprise payment infrastructure on top of XRPL.

Core Technology:
- Consensus: Federated Byzantine Agreement (FBA) — no mining, no staking
- Settlement Speed: 3-5 seconds per transaction
- Transaction Cost: Fractions of a cent (fees are burned)
- Total Supply: 100 billion XRP (pre-mined, fixed — no new supply creation)
- Escrow: ~46 billion XRP held in cryptographic escrow, releasing 1 billion/month (unused portions re-locked)

Key Use Cases:
- Cross-border payment rails (Ripple Payments, formerly ODL)
- Stablecoin infrastructure (RLUSD on XRPL)
- Institutional settlement and liquidity bridging
- Native AMM liquidity provisioning (XLS-30d)
- Tokenized real-world assets (emerging)

Notable Partnerships (2026):
- Deutsche Bank, Aviva, AUDD (first government-licensed stablecoin on XRPL, Australia ASIC)
- PayPal (historical), MoneyGram (historical), Franklin Templeton
- Ripple Payments platform: $100B+ processed volume

Tokenomics & Operations

XRP has 100B total supply (pre-mined, no mining). Ripple holds ~46B XRP in escrow, releasing 1B/month with unused portions re-locked. This creates predictable but significant potential sell pressure. XRPL features native AMMs (XLS-30d amendment) — a protocol-level mechanism for holders to earn yield through LP fee collection. Transaction fees are burned, providing deflationary pressure. The escrow release schedule is the dominant tokenomics factor — Ripple's willingness to sell or re-lock determines effective supply expansion.
